The lab

Small, reproducible experiments in machine learning, planning and control. The networks and planners are written from scratch and run in your browser. Every result comes with the protocol that produced it, and the negative results are published too.

System One / System Two

Can a tiny network, trained by a slow planner, make most decisions at a fraction of the planner's cost, and know when to hand control back?

A fast network (System One) proposes every move with a confidence. When it is unsure, or when a cheap safety check rejects its move, a slow planner (System Two) decides instead, and that decision becomes a new training example. The same pipeline runs on five very different problems.

  • Snake With a cheap safety check, the hybrid reaches 98% of the planner's score for 8.8× less compute, just short of the 10× target.
  • Lander A hand-written autopilot beats the network at the same cost: sometimes a rule is enough.
  • Warehouse 16 robots: with less compute than the planner's cheaper setting, the hybrid delivers 89.4 loads against 60.1; the 10× target is missed.
  • Racing The network alone drives 99.9% as far as the planner, for 1,164× less compute.
  • Quadruped A 3D walking robot you can push. With its safety check, the network walks 91% as far as the planner for 82× less compute, while agreeing with it on only 41% of decisions.

Handwriting pad

Can a network with 22,298 parameters read handwritten letters as well as a classic recogniser that compares your letter with every stored example?

Write one uppercase letter at a time with your finger, like on a car's touchpad. Both recognisers read it in your browser, and the page shows how much arithmetic each one spent.

  • Result On 20 writers it never saw, the network reads 83.9% of letters right, the classic recogniser 83.2%, for 1,302× less arithmetic. The 90% target is missed.

How experiments are run

  1. Targets first. What would count as success is written down before measuring, and never changed afterwards.
  2. Separate data. Design decisions use development seeds or writers; final numbers use held-out test ones, used only for the final studies.
  3. Repeated runs. Every final result that depends on training is the mean of 5 independent training runs, with 95% intervals.
  4. Feasibility first. Before a long study, a short test with go/no-go criteria decides whether it is worth running; when a study goes ahead anyway, that is stated.
  5. Everything logged. Every design decision is recorded with its evidence, and negative results are reported like positive ones.
  6. From scratch. No machine-learning libraries: networks, backpropagation, planners and environments are written in TypeScript (the quadruped's 3D physics uses the Rapier engine).
